# Recommendations workspace: three alternatives (2026-11-14)

Replaces the dense one-screen table in `mockups/interface.svg`. All three use the same synthetic round-1 data (19 recommendations: Open 13 · Applied 2 · Kept in place 3 · Outdated 1, plus 31 no space), the same selected move (#1, 4410281 Brake pads, front – VW Golf, G-14-04-02 → G-14-01-04 BEST, 61 picks; the landing page shows the same move), the same toolbar (plan plate, 8-step flow bar with checks, launcher, avatar), and aisle G drawn as a bin wall with the lifted bin, numbered markers and the violet arrow to the dashed target.

Files: `A-review-queue.svg`, `B-list-detail.svg`, `C-wall-first.svg`, generated by `gen_interface.py` (imports helpers and `defs.svgfrag` from `../../landing-redesign/`). Run `python3 gen_interface.py` to rebuild.

## A · Review queue (one at a time)

Each recommendation gets the whole stage: part, a large FROM → TO, three fact tiles that answer "why" (61 picks / L4 now / L1 best), the partner hint, and Apply · Other location ▾ · Keep in place · Manual move… with their Alt shortcuts. The aisle wall beside it shows the move, and "If applied" shows the effect. A strip of numbered discs above it (grouped by rule, one disc per recommendation, with its state) is both the progress bar and the way to jump to any item, and Previous / Next steps through the open ones. It is the calmest layout and puts the reason first, which suits the landing's "Every move comes with a reason". The cost is the overview: you cannot compare rows side by side or scan all targets at once, and for 19+ items Apply all and the Outdated/Kept lists rely on the strip.

## B · List and detail (master/detail)

The list on the left is reduced to what you scan: number, class swatch, part, FROM → TO with BEST, picks. State tabs with counts (Open · Applied · Kept in place · Outdated · No space) replace the rule tabs, and the rules become bands inside the list. The detail on the right gives the reason as one plain sentence with the numbers in bold, the wall crop, the move line with its effect, and one action bar at the bottom. This is the most familiar and easiest to build, and it keeps the overview. The reason is less visible per row than in A, and the detail pane has less room for the wall than C.

## C · Wall first (list as a rail)

The open aisle is the main picture: a 4-section wall where every move touching the aisle is lifted and numbered, and the selected one draws its arrow and a hover note. You switch aisles with the letter plates (each with its count of moves), and the rail on the right holds the state counts, this aisle's moves, and the other aisles plus No space. The selected move's reason and actions sit in a strip under the wall. This makes the space itself the argument, which is where the Bin Wall direction is strongest. But moves between aisles (rule 2 out to H, rule 3 to a partner's aisle) show only one end of the move, and reviewing by rule or by state means more navigation.

## Recommendation

**A, with B's list as a toggle.** A makes the reason impossible to miss and has the fewest elements on the screen. Its queue strip already carries the counts per state. A "List" toggle that opens B's compact list (same selection, same actions) covers the moments when the planner wants an overview or wants to compare many rows before Apply all. C's aisle-centred view fits better as the "Show on the Locations wall" destination than as the main workspace.

## Deviations and open points for the spines

- Numbers run 1–19 across rules (not "#1 of 9" per rule) so that a wall marker is never ambiguous. EXPERIENCE.md currently ranks per rule.
- B uses state tabs (Open / Applied / Kept in place / Outdated / No space) with rule bands inside. EXPERIENCE.md has tabs per rule plus No space, Kept in place and Outdated. A and C show the same counts as a strip and as tiles.
- Since Apply on the selected item is the one primary button, Run recommendations is drawn as secondary in all three.
- Reset… is moved into the "⋯" overflow menu in the header.
- A's three fact tiles and the "If applied" line are a richer version of the recommendation detail ("Effect if applied" is already an [ASSUMPTION] in EXPERIENCE.md).
