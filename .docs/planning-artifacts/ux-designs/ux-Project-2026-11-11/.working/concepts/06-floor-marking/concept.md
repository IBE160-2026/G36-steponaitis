# 06 Floor Marking

**Thesis.** The tool reads like the floor it changes. It refuses colour-coded pills on a white dashboard.

**Own world.** Graphite shell `#1A1E22`, work surface `#E9E7E2`, sealed-concrete map `#B9B6AF`. Each tape colour means one thing: yellow `#F2C200` for walkways and the primary action, green `#23743F` for best location, red/white `#C0302A` for keep in place, blue `#1D5DB0` for the route. Type is Overpass, with Saira Stencil One for aisle letters only.

**Signature move.** ISO 7010 shape grammar: rule 1 is an information square, rule 2 a warning triangle, rule 3 two linked squares. Keep in place is a prohibition circle, outdated a struck-through sign, no space a stop octagon. The same signs appear on the map, the list and the print.

**Warehouse layout.** A top-down plan with a taped cross walkway. Stencilled aisle letters carry their occupancy %. Each location is a beam-label cell, with levels shown as rows. Class is shown by tone plus a glyph, and an empty cell has a diagonal line. The selected move is a taped arrow along the walkways with its sign at the head.

**Navigation.** A dark rail holds the plan and data-as-of date. The top bar shows the flow as a walkway. The Directory (G) is a wayfinding board: zones 1–5, arrows for back, here and ahead, and stage 2 marked under construction.

**Honest risk.** Showing levels as rows makes a dense hybrid, not a literal plan, and signs crowd where recommendations cluster.

**Trust:** every move shows its rule as a sign and its reason in numbers, on screen and on paper.
